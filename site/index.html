Your compliance has a TTL.

OpenAISF is an open conformance framework for AI safety and security in which conformance is a state with a heartbeat, rather than an event that produced a document.

20 domains · 112 controls 677 requirements · 0 gaps CC-BY-4.0 / Apache-2.0 RFC open for comment
An AI system is OpenAISF-conformant at tier T for as long as it keeps producing signed evidence that satisfies tier T's applicable controls.

Every AI governance regime in use today answers “were you compliant when somebody looked?” OpenAISF answers “are you compliant right now?” — and makes the answer checkable by a stranger.

What it does not claim

Each of the following is a claim OpenAISF does not make.

It does not promise no agent goes rogue. Nothing can, and a standard implying otherwise would be destroyed by the first incident. It requires that agent authority is bounded, that departure is detected by detectors proven to fire, that it can be contained by a kill-switch that has been exercised and timed, that damage is recoverable — and that all four are proven on a clock, or the certificate expires.

It does not claim to be the first machine-readable AI compliance work. By 2026 that is simply false. OSCAL, Policy Cards, AIP and DEMM-Bench all exist and are cited. OpenAISF exports OSCAL rather than competing with it.

It does not pretend incumbent assurance is naïve. SOC 2 Type II examines a period. ISO 42001 runs surveillance audits. The EU AI Act already requires post-market monitoring. The difference lies in four specific places, and the RFC states them precisely.

How a lie gets caught

Evidence has two planes. The control plane says a policy is configured. The data plane says it was enforced, from live traffic. Claim a policy is on while a day of traffic crossed the enforcement point with zero decisions recorded, and the run says:

D07-C01  [fail]  declared enabled, but 184203 requests crossed the
                 enforcement point with 0 decisions recorded. A policy
                 that never fired under live traffic was not operating.

lease: revoked

No auditor was involved, and an attestation cannot resolve it. Fabrication is disqualifying — it blocks even where the control is only recommended, and revokes rather than degrades. Obligation level governs whether a shortfall matters. It does not govern whether a lie counts as a lie.

Two companion rules: a missing signal is failure, not silence, because a compliant system and a broken pipeline emit the same silence; and freshness expires on its own, per control, capped by tier.

A badge is a function of the reader's clock

A conformance statement carries its own stale_after and expires_at. A verifier needs the statement, a key and a clock — not the catalog, not your evidence, not your cooperation, and not a registry that could be captured or pressured.

✗ TruCert · OpenAISF-T2 · expired

This badge no longer asserts conformance. Nobody revoked it;
the lease simply ran out.

No lease outlives its tier ceiling, so a subject cannot construct a statement that never needs renewing. The log is hash-chained: rewrite a past entry to upgrade yourself and verification stops. And assurance decay propagates downstream — a provider who lets their badge lapse degrades every customer depending on it. No existing framework models that.

Provable coverage, and honest exclusion

Every framework claims it maps to the others. Mapping outward from your own controls can only show what you found. OpenAISF inverts it: each regime is inventoried to its atomic requirements, and every one must be covered by named controls or excluded with a written reason. No third state. A gap fails the build.

Three names, three jobs

CertifierA role defined by the standardAnyone meeting its requirements

A system can reach any tier, including T4, with no money changing hands and no certifier involved. That is not a concession; it is how a standard spreads. The Certifier requirements are published before any certifier launches, so the role demonstrably predates its first occupant.

Try it

openaisf scope   --context system.yaml --tier T2
openaisf check   --context system.yaml --evidence ./evidence --tier T2
openaisf publish --context system.yaml --evidence ./evidence --log log.jsonl
openaisf verify  --log log.jsonl --system urn:… --key signer.pub

Exit 0 means conformant. There is also an MCP server, so an agent can read the catalog and check its own conformance while it builds — though it deliberately cannot submit evidence, sign or publish. An agent asserting its own compliance is a claim, not evidence.
